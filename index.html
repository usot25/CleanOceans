Why we exist

The ocean regulates our climate, feeds billions and makes the air breathable. We treat it as the last stop for our waste. The Clean Oceans Campaign exists to change where plastic ends up — by stopping it upstream, cleaning what already reached the coast and raising a generation that refuses to make the same mistake.

The problem

From street to sea

Most ocean plastic starts on land. It travels a predictable route — which means it can be stopped at predictable points.

  1. 01 — Street & drain

    Litter becomes runoff

    Wrappers, bottles and bags dropped in streets are washed into storm drains with the first heavy rain.

  2. 02 — River

    Rivers carry it downstream

    Waterways act as conveyor belts. This is where interception is cheapest and most effective — one barrier can protect an entire coastline.

  3. 03 — Coast

    Shorelines catch the overflow

    Beaches, mangroves and estuaries trap debris, smothering habitats that nurseries of fish depend on.

  4. 04 — Open ocean

    It breaks down — but never disappears

    Sunlight and waves fragment plastic into microplastics that enter the food chain and persist for generations.

    Questions

    Good to know

    Where does my donation go?

    Donations fund river barriers, cleanup kits, mangrove nurseries and free school materials. We publish a quarterly breakdown of spending and results.

    Why focus on rivers rather than the open ocean?

    Most ocean plastic starts on land and travels through waterways. Intercepting it upstream is simpler and cheaper than recovering fragments once they are dispersed at sea.

    Why do plastic-pollution figures differ between sources?

    Researchers measure different things — plastic entering all aquatic ecosystems versus the ocean alone — using different models. We quote UNEP and link every figure so you can check it.

    Can I volunteer if there’s no cleanup near me?

    Yes. Choose “Volunteer” in the form and we’ll send you a self-guided cleanup kit and logging guide so you can organise one locally.

    Are the lesson plans really free?

    Yes — free for schools, libraries and community groups.
